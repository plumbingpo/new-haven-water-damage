No part of restoring a water-damaged building matters more than structural drying, yet it is also where untrained crews make the most mistakes. Extraction takes care of standing water, but drywall, framing, subflooring, insulation, concrete and other building materials stay loaded with trapped moisture. This trapped moisture must be scientifically removed through controlled evaporation and dehumidification to prevent the mold growth, wood rot, material degradation and odor problems that develop when structures remain wet.

The structural drying technicians at New Haven Restoration are IICRC-certified and trained in psychrometry, the science of how temperature, humidity and moisture in air relate. That knowledge lets them work out where equipment goes, what temperature to hold and how air should move so evaporation is as effective as possible on every job. Plaster, concrete, hardwood and drywall each dry at their own rate, so each needs its own approach to get down to an acceptable moisture level with no added damage.

Every day of the drying process, we check moisture using thermal imaging cameras, thermo-hygrometers and calibrated moisture meters. We document moisture readings throughout the drying process, creating a detailed drying log that shows progressive moisture reduction in all affected materials. Each day we use those numbers to reposition or retune the equipment so drying stays efficient. We call a job dry when every affected material meets the moisture standard in the IICRC S500 guidelines, and never just because a surface feels dry.

Signs You Need Structural Drying in New Haven

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Baseboards or door trim that swell, warp or pull away from the wall
  • Blistered paint and lifting wallpaper on a wall that got wet
  • Wallboard with water stains, puffiness or a spongy feel
  • Ceilings, floors or walls that still read high on a moisture meter following a leak
  • Musty odors that continue after extraction, which suggests trapped moisture
  • Hardwood floors that are cupping, crowning or showing gaps between boards
  • Materials breaking down in a spot that got wet before and was not dried by professionals

What a Professional Brings to Structural Drying

Drying a structure properly takes science, and guesswork has no place in it. The leading cause of secondary mold after a water event is setting out a few fans and trusting they will be enough. Professional structural drying requires understanding how different materials absorb and release moisture, calculating the grain depression (moisture removal capacity) each situation needs, and monitoring daily to confirm drying is progressing properly. Lacking that understanding, people either stop too soon and leave moisture for mold, or dry too hard and harm wood and other materials.

Consumer products and the equipment professionals dry structures with have very little in common. Commercial LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers remove 10 to 20 times more moisture from the air than residential dehumidifiers, and high-velocity air movers create the precise airflow patterns needed to accelerate evaporation from structural surfaces. Machines like these cost a great deal and take training to use well, which explains why professional results are so much better than a do-it-yourself effort.

How to Prevent Structural Drying Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Plan for daily access so readings can be taken and equipment adjusted
  • Until a technician removes the equipment, keep it running day and night without breaks
  • Trust the moisture meter readings, because surfaces can feel dry while moisture remains deep in materials
  • Every hour of run time matters, so resist switching the machines off for some quiet
  • Keep windows and doors closed in areas being dried so the dehumidifiers stay efficient
  • Do not place personal items on top of drying equipment or block the airflow from air movers
