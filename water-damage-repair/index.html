Water Damage Repair Prevention Tips

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • File the written scope and photos of the completed repair with your household papers.
  • No wall or floor should be sealed by any contractor until you have seen final moisture readings.
  • Once a year, renew the caulk at sinks, showers and tubs to keep water from entering the wall.
  • Rebuilt bathroom walls last longer when the exhaust fan runs through and after each shower.
  • Do not paint or patch over a water stain until the source is fixed.
  • Photograph damaged materials before they are removed in case your insurer asks later.

How We Handle Water Damage Repair

Our systematic approach delivers quality results on every project. Here's how we handle water damage repair from start to finish:

1

Confirm It Is Dry

We take final moisture readings on framing, subfloor and remaining drywall. Only when each material reads normal does rebuilding start, which keeps damp spots from being sealed inside.

2

Put the Scope in Writing

We hand you a list, room by room, covering what gets replaced, planned materials and the likely schedule. When there is an open claim, the scope is coordinated with your adjuster.

3

Rebuild the Structure

Open wall cavities get new insulation, the subfloor is fixed, and drywall goes up, then is taped and finished. After that we install trim, cabinetry and floors, ordering the work so completed surfaces are not damaged.

4

Final Finishes and Walkthrough

We match texture, prime and paint, and reinstall doors, baseboards and hardware. Together we then walk through each room, and every listed item is corrected before the job is closed.

When to Call for Water Damage Repair in New Haven

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Walking over the formerly wet area, you feel soft spots in the floor.
  • A leak overhead left the ceiling drywall cracked, sagging or falling apart.
  • A door frame or slab swelled, so the door rubs or fails to close.
  • Drywall was cut out during drying and the wall cavities are still open.
  • Laminate or vinyl plank edges are swollen, curled or separating at the seams.
  • You see brown stains, peeling or bubbles in paint along the path the water took.
  • Water under the tile left cracked grout lines or tiles that rock when stepped on.
