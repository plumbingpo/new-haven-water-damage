Crawl Space Water Removal in New Haven

A flooded crawl space rarely announces itself. Most people find it when the floors feel cold and damp, a musty smell comes up through the vents, or someone opens the access hatch and sees a pond. Usually that means days of moisture in the joists already, plus insulation that is wet all the way through. We pump the space dry, remove the ruined material and get the wood dried while it is still sound.

Once the standing water is gone, the job is only halfway done. Wood framing takes water in gradually and gives it up just as gradually, which is why we meter the joists, beams and subfloor and leave dehumidifiers on until readings drop, typically 3 to 5 days. We also look for where the water came from, a leaking supply line, a drain, or groundwater after rain, so it does not fill back up.

What It Looks Like

  • An increase in insects and pests that like damp, dark spaces
  • Hardwood boards upstairs starting to cup, with edges higher than the centers
  • Flooring overhead that seems chilly, clammy, spongy or springy underfoot
  • Standing water or mud on the ground when you open the access hatch
  • Dark water marks and chalky deposits partway up the block or concrete
  • Rust on ductwork straps, pipe hangers or metal support posts
What We Do

What Happens After You Call

1

Inspect and Make Safe

Nobody enters until the space has been screened for shock risks, hanging ducts and contamination. Next comes charting the extent of the water and metering the joists and subfloor for moisture.

2

Remove Standing Water

We run pumps and extraction lines until the pooled water is gone, low pockets and trenches along the footings included. Mud and silt left on the liner are cleared at the same time.

3

Remove Wet Materials

Soaked insulation, ruined vapor barrier and debris are bagged and carried out. Our crew then wipes down the uncovered framing and applies antimicrobial to spots with growth or a high chance of it.

4

Final Drying and Checks

Compact fans and dehumidification work in the crawl space as long as it takes for the framing to test normal. A final meter check follows, and you receive the numbers along with steps to prevent a repeat.

The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

First day

Puddles Form Underneath

Water collects in the low spots and soaks into the soil. Humidity in the space jumps within hours as the insulation pulls water into itself.

Hours 24 to 48

Mold Gets Started

Mold can start on damp joists and paper faced insulation. Soaked insulation gains weight and starts to sag off the floor above.

Day 3 to 7

Moisture Moves Upstairs

Damp air rises into the living space. The lumber goes on taking in moisture, a stale smell spreads and flooring can begin to cup.

After a week

Wood Starts Rotting

Decay begins in any framing that never got a chance to dry. Sill plates and joists lose firmness, nails and hangers corrode, and termites along with other pests move toward the moisture.

What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

Contaminated Water

What collects beneath a home is frequently dirty. If it came from a drain pipe or from waste laden groundwater it is rated Category 2 or 3, so we suit up and disinfect.

Pests Drawn to Moisture

Rodents, carpenter ants and termites all seek out moist lumber and soggy ground. Getting moisture levels down takes away a main reason for them to stay.

Wet Wiring and Ducts

Most crawl spaces carry wiring, junction boxes and HVAC ducts. Wires under water can shock someone, and wet duct wrap pushes a moldy smell through every vent.

Rot You Cannot See

Decay tends to begin along the upper edge of the framing or where it meets the sill, out of view from the access opening. We probe the wood to find soft spots.
